Questions people ask.

Short answers first. If yours isn’t here, email us or open an issue on GitHub.

Basics

Is DriftFetch really free?

Yes. It’s MIT-licensed open source with no ads, trials or paid features.

Do I need an account?

No. There’s nothing to sign up for.

Mac or Linux?

Not yet. DriftFetch is built and tested for Windows 10 and 11 only.

Which sites does it work with?

Sites supported by yt-dlp and gallery-dl, such as YouTube, Reddit, Vimeo and Internet Archive. See the supported sites page.

Downloading

Where do my files go?

To Downloads\DriftFetch by default, in a folder per site. You can change the folder, sorting and file names in Settings.

How do I download a playlist or album?

Copy its link. DriftFetch shows every item with a thumbnail and you tick what you want.

Can I pick the quality?

By default it takes the best quality available. You can set a maximum resolution or a preferred format in Settings.

Will it download the same thing twice?

No. Links you’ve already saved are caught and marked “Already in list”.

When something goes wrong

It says “Rate limited”. Is that an error?

No. The site asked DriftFetch to slow down. It waits as long as it was told to and carries on by itself.

It says “Sign-in needed”.

The content is members-only or age-restricted. Sign in to that site once from the app and retry.

A site that used to work stopped working.

Sites change often. DriftFetch updates its engines automatically; if it’s still broken after a day or two, report it on GitHub.

Privacy and safety

Does DriftFetch collect any data?

No. There’s no DriftFetch server. The app only talks to the sites you download from and to GitHub for engine updates.

Is it safe to install?

The code is public and every release has a checksum you can verify. See the download page for how.

Is downloading allowed?

Only save content you have the right to keep, and follow each site’s terms. DriftFetch doesn’t remove DRM or get around paywalls.
